# 1 Motivations

Figure 1: The DM research map. Due to the absence of a conclusive experimental signal, it is unlikely to determine the properties of the DM particle. There are multiple approaches available to test a DM theory; however, by using the advantages of null detection results, we can effectively explore potential
solutions through the integration of these approaches.
The nature of dark matter (DM) remains one of the biggest mysteries of this century, and extensive global efforts are dedicated to searching for its non-gravitational interaction. Despite significant experimental advancements in recent decades, the particle properties of DM remain unknown. Fig. 1 illustrates an approach to test a DM theory by starting with a DM Lagrangian coupled to an SM Lagrangian and comparing experimental observables to data. This Lagrangian-based approach allows us to incorporate all experimental constraints and further explore the potential parameter space. The Planck relic density can serve as a DM signal, predicting detectable signals in human instruments. Alternatively, we can study possible excesses against the background in DM indirect detection, DM direct detection, collider searches, and cosmology experiments. By improving our understanding of the background, we can better identify regions where a potential DM signal may exist.
Bridging DM models and experimental data is a challenging task. Fig. 2 illustrates the workflow for studying the nature of DM. A comprehensive study encompasses all steps from (a) to (f), although some DM research focuses on only a portion or individual steps. The blue boxes (a) to (b) represent the particle model-building process. Conversely, researchers working on DM indirect detection, represented by the green section from (c) to (f), consider more astrophysical parameters than just a particle model. Interestingly, those working on the blue boxes are usually unfamiliar with the green sections, and vice versa. To benefit the community, my collaborators and I have developed useful codes like LikeDM (download the code from https://likedm.hepforge.org/) and BayesFITS. These codes facilitate combination studies of both particle and astrophysical model parameter spaces.
